Holly,

Apple rejected 1.0 again last night. Different reason than the first time.
I want you to read exactly what they wrote, because it sounds worse than it
is.

Their words:

"The app primarily features astrology, horoscopes, palm reading, fortune
telling or zodiac reports that duplicate the content and functionality of
similar apps that are already widely available.

These app features may be useful, informative or entertaining, and the app
may include features or characteristics that distinguish it. However, there
are already enough of these apps on the App Store.

Next Steps: We encourage you to reconsider the app concept and submit a new
app that provides a unique experience not already found on the App Store."

That is guideline 4.3(b), their spam rule for crowded categories. It is a
form letter. Nobody at Apple sat with the app and decided your work was
ordinary.

Here is what actually happened, and it is on us.

I pulled the notes we submitted alongside the app. They open with the
sentence "Tarotdoxa is an AI tarot-reading app," then spend the rest on
sign-in steps and billing disclosures answering the first rejection. Not
one word about the writing, the audio, the journal, the real astronomy, or
your twenty years. Our keywords were divination, oracle, psychic, mystic.
The description said Tarot about twenty times.

So everything Apple looked at told them "generic tarot app." We never made
the case, and they ruled on what we handed them.

The rule they cited has a door in it that I missed until today. Their own
text:

"Certain kinds of apps, such as dating, flashlight, sound effects,
wallpaper, simple timers, and fortune telling, are well established on the
App Store and we will not accept new submissions unless they offer a
meaningfully different or improved experience."

Meaningfully different is allowed. We just never argued it.

Here is what I changed today. None of it touches the app. Same build, same
code, nothing you have tested is different.

Review notes to Apple. Rewritten from scratch. They now lead with what the
software does before tarot is mentioned at all: every reading is written
fresh instead of pulled from a table of card meanings, any reading can be
narrated in your voice, the charts and daily sky are computed from real
astronomy at the moment someone asks rather than shipped as a canned table,
there is an encrypted journal, every reading keeps its own conversation
open for months, and the app tracks which cards keep returning to a person
across their whole history. Your twenty-year corpus is in there as the
reason the voice is yours and not a generic model.

Keywords. Removed divination, oracle, psychic, and mystic. Those four were
doing real damage. They are the exact vocabulary of the category Apple says
is full. Added journal, audio, voice, reflection, and birth chart, which
are things we actually built and never once mentioned.

Subtitle. Was "Tarot in a real reader's voice." Now "Written for you, read
aloud." The app name already says tarot. Spending our only other line
saying it again was wasted.

Description. Rewrote the top third. It used to open "Tarotdoxa gives you
real Tarot readings." It now opens:

"Tarotdoxa writes you a reading. Not a card meaning pulled from a list, an
actual piece of writing about your spread, composed fresh every time and
read aloud in her voice if you want to hear it."

Then a plain list of what someone gets: the writing, the audio, the
journal, the follow-up conversation, the real astronomy, the pattern
tracking, and gifting. I also put the "for entertainment purposes only"
line back into the public description, which had quietly dropped out
somewhere along the way. Pricing and the legal links are untouched.

I sent Apple a direct reply as well, saying our earlier notes failed to
make the case, that the miss was ours, here is the case, and offering a
call if they want us to demonstrate any of it.

It went back into review this morning at 8:52. The last two rounds each
took about a day.

Two more things worth knowing.

We are holding Android. It is approved and sitting ready to go, but if
Apple ends up forcing a real change to the app, I do not want the two
versions drifting apart. Both still go live together.

And if this one bounces too, there is a formal appeal we have not used yet.
I would rather not need it, and I want to be straight with you that a
similar app lost that appeal earlier this year. That one had none of what
we built, and it never made the argument either.

Nothing about the app changed. Only what Apple was told about it.
